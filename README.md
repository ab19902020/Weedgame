# Green County · Berkshire sandbox (v0.41.1)

A free-roam, 1:1 slice of Berkshire, 219 km² from Reading and Caversham in the west to Bracknell in the east, plus the whole M4 across it from Junction 12 (Theale) to Junction 8/9 (Holyport): Reading town centre, Cemetery Junction, Earley, Woodley, Winnersh, Wokingham and Bracknell, and every village, lane, footpath, lake and river between them (Sonning, Twyford, Charvil, Hurst, Dinton Pastures, Sindlesham, Shinfield, Arborfield, Binfield and more). It starts in **sandbox mode**: you control Cal or Leon Ward and can explore indefinitely. There are no missions, objectives, phone calls, deliveries or sales. The cannabis-empire layer from the v0.3–v0.5 prototype will sit on this world later; its save data is carried along untouched.

## Play

* **Android**: install `builds/Green-County-0.35.0-dev.apk`. It was built in another container from v0.21.1's compiled wrapper and signed with that container's development key (SHA-256 2e5c0581…, the key of v0.19.0, v0.21.0, v0.22.0, the Road Atlas build of v0.23.0, v0.25.0, v0.26.0, v0.27.0, v0.28.0, v0.29.0, v0.30.0, v0.31.0, v0.32.0, v0.33.0 and v0.34.0), so those update in place. Every other build used another key: the original key of v0.20.x and v0.21.1, or the key of Grok's builds of v0.23.0, v0.24.0 and v0.25.0 (d5f3f7bb…). Coming from any of those, uninstall Green County first (saved games on the device go with it). Landscape only.
* **Touch**: on foot, the left thumb walks (a stick appears wherever you touch) and the right drags the camera; Run, Jump, Hit and Pick up sit bottom right, with Get in / Board / Exit above them. Driving, the left thumb steers: Settings → Steering offers a **Steering wheel** you drag sideways (the default; proportional, as Real Racing's and CarX's wheels), **Buttons** (left and right, as GTA's and Real Racing's touch zones: a tap is a small correction, a hold full lock, and letting go centres the wheel) or **Tilt**. Settings → Handling: **Easy** (assists on, the default) or **Realistic** (fewer assists). The right thumb works the GAS and BRAKE pedals (rock between them without lifting), plus Handbrake and Horn. Holding BRAKE at a standstill engages reverse after a moment.
* **Browser**: `npm run serve`, then open http://localhost:8080. Chrome or Edge recommended; WebGPU is used when available, otherwise WebGL2.

Touch controls (landscape): drag on the **left side** to move (a floating stick appears under your thumb). Driving: push the stick up for throttle, pull it back to brake and then reverse, and steer sideways. Drag on the **right side** to orbit the camera. **Run** (on foot) doubles as the **handbrake** in a vehicle (it locks the rear wheels, so it swings the tail round at speed). **Get in / Exit** works on any parked car. **Horn** appears while driving. **Swap** switches between Cal and Leon wherever each is. **Map** opens the full map, **Dev** the developer panel.
Keyboard: WASD or arrows, Shift run, F get in/out (and hail a taxi, get in the back, ask to stop), Space handbrake, H horn, C/V camera, Tab swap, M map, N phone, P dev panel, Q/E orbit.
Controller (DualShock or any standard-mapping pad): left stick moves and steers, right stick looks. Driving: R2 throttle, L2 brake and reverse, Cross/Square/L1 handbrake, R1 camera view, Circle or L3 horn. On foot: Cross run, Square jump, Circle/R2 hit, L1/R1 pick up a parcel. Triangle gets in or out, boards a bus or train, and requests the next stop. Options or PS opens the menu, and Share or the touchpad opens the Road Atlas. On the Atlas, the stick pans, L1/R1 zoom and Triangle drops a waypoint. D-pad: up the phone, down swaps brothers, left/right turn the camera 90°. R3 recentres the camera. In menus, the d-pad moves, Cross selects and Circle goes back.
Start menu: **Enter free roam**, **Road Atlas** (search places, drop a pin anywhere and follow the route on the minimap and in-car sat nav) and **Settings** (minimap orientation and size, route colour).

The developer panel can set the hour and clock speed, force any weather (clear, broken, overcast, drizzle, rain, mist, clearing), soak or dry the roads, stage a collision for the police to attend, run extra simulation steps, teleport to districts and test spots, show the performance overlay, save a performance log (written to app storage on Android), and switch between WebGPU and WebGL2.

URL options (browser): `?backend=webgl|webgpu`, `?quality=low|medium|high`, `?perf` (overlay), `?time=17.5`, `?weather=rain`, `?scale=120`, `?sim=4`, `?r0=…&r1=…` (streaming radii), `?post=0`, `?ao=0`, `?ibl=0`, `?grass=0|1`, `?ssr=0`, `?fsr=0` (no upscaling), `?skybake=0` (per-pixel sky), `?tm=aces` (look A/B), `?nobatch` (render batching off).

## What runs

* **World**: 3,730 streamed 256 m tiles (the whole study area and a 1 km corridor along the M4 from J12 to J8/9, 219 km²) in two LOD rings, built from OSM and EA LIDAR. Each building footprint becomes its own closed mesh with gabled or hipped roofs, chimneys, procedural façades, front doors, shopfronts and lit windows. Carriageways, kerbed pavements, paths and plazas are seamless polygons; markings include centre and lane lines, stop and give-way lines, zebras with zig-zags, and studs at signalised crossings. Bridges and flyovers have decks, parapets and piers on separate levels. Also streamed: street lamps, trees, walls, hedges, fences, shop fascias, rails, bus stops and shelters, canopies, the Cemetery Junction gateway and Reading Town Hall.
* **Roads** (`src/roadProfile.js`, `src/roadDetail.js`, applied when the network loads): every bridge deck spans between the embankment crests in the LIDAR ground and rises only for headroom over the road or railway beneath. Roads climb to a deck at their class's grade (motorways 3 %), on earth embankments with abutments. Holes in the ground under roads (culverts, underpasses) are filled. The M4's dropped River Loddon spans are rejoined, and one-way carriageways that leave the map no longer send traffic back the wrong way. Motorways have a hard shoulder and 3.65 m lanes, a concrete step barrier in the central reservation and red/amber/white studs. Dual carriageways get a steel safety fence, and so do the verges of high embankments. Markings follow TSRGD: hazard lines before junctions, single broken give-way lines at roundabouts, broken motorway edge lines where a slip road joins or leaves, and zig-zags only at stand-alone crossings. Signal stop lines and heads come only from traffic arriving at a signal. The drawn road always matches the height traffic drives at: decks carry on as approach slabs while a road leaving a bridge stands above the ground, the ground is cut down where it stands above a road (underpasses, the gaps between bridge spans, cuttings narrower than the 4 m terrain grid), and a deck carries any piece of road the ground under it falls away from (a slip road climbing beside a cutting); the player drives on those decks too. Bridge piers stand clear of the roads, rivers and railways beneath. Bus stops mapped in the carriageway stand at the kerb on the side their buses use, with a BUS STOP box where buses pass; every flag and shelter is fitted onto the drawn footway, clear of the road and of buildings (`npm run stops` → `www/world/stops.json`).
* **Trains and stations** (`src/trains.js`, `src/stations.js`, `tools/rail/build_rail.mjs` → `www/world/rail.json`): the Reading–Waterloo line (Reading, Earley, Winnersh Triangle, Winnersh, Wokingham, Bracknell, Martins Heron) and the Reading–Basingstoke line (Reading, Reading West) run trains every few minutes on the left-hand track each way, stopping at every station and reversing at the ends of the map. The Great Western main line east of Reading has four tracks, paired by use: stopping trains run on the relief (slow) lines on the north side between Reading and Twyford, calling at both, and fast trains run non-stop on the main lines on the south side through Reading and Twyford to the edges of the map; on every line the up (towards London) and down services each keep to their own left-hand track, and no two lines share a track. Every station has a lit platform beside each running track (one at a terminus) with tactile paving, a yellow line, shelter, benches, lamps and name boards, level with the train's floor; where a station stands in a cutting the ground is cut back down to the track under its platforms. Walk onto a platform and press Triangle (or Board) when a train stands there to get on; the doors open on the platform side, the cabin shows the next station, Triangle requests the stop, and at the end of the line everyone gets off. Waiting passengers board and alight too.
* **Procedural detail and street life**: houses get canted bay windows (some two-storey), door hoods on brackets, fascias, soffits, gutters and downpipes, bargeboards, ridge tiles, chimney pots and a plinth. Streets get wheelie bins, telegraph poles with line wires and drops to the houses, pillar boxes, litter bins and benches. All of it is built inside the existing tile meshes, so it adds no draw calls.
* **Image and frame rate**: the scene renders at a dynamic internal resolution and is upscaled with FSR 1 (edge-adaptive upsampling + sharpening), so the image stays sharp while the scale moves to hold 60 fps (30 fps evenly paced as the floor). The cloud sky is baked into a cube map four times a second instead of raymarched per pixel; cars beyond 70 m use a light LOD.
* **A steady picture** (v0.21): nothing should shimmer or flicker as you move.
  - **Shadows**: the sun's shadow map follows you in whole shadow-map texels, measured in the light's own frame (`main.js placeSun`). It used to slide under the world by fractions of a texel every frame, so every shadow edge crawled as you walked or drove. It now also turns with the sun in quarter-degree steps rather than a little every frame. The normal offset is two shadow texels instead of 0.6 m, so shadows meet the feet and wheels that cast them, and bins, bollards and animals cast shadows again.
  - **Windows and shopfronts**: frames, glazing bars, sills, doors, shop mullions, fascias and cladding ribs are drawn per pixel. They are now anti-aliased against the pixel's footprint on the wall, and the rooms seen through distant windows settle to their average colour. Before, detail thinner than a pixel snapped on and off as the camera moved.
  - **Ambient occlusion**: GTAO's noise pattern is smoothed by two depth-aware blur passes, so contact shadows no longer crawl.
  - **Reflections**: screen-space reflections are kept to surfaces facing up (wet roads, puddles, bonnets). They shimmered on upright glass.
  - **Trees**: trees fade out over the last 70 m before the tree draw distance, instead of whole tiles of them popping on the skyline.
  - **Resolution**: dynamic resolution only sharpens again after two quiet checks in a row, in small steps.
* **Weapons** (v0.32, `src/weaponsData.js`, `src/combat.js`, `src/armIK.js`, `src/fx.js`; models `www/weapons/`, recordings `www/sfx/guns/`, credits in `www/weapons/SOURCES.txt`): both brothers carry all ten, with unlimited ammunition (magazines still need reloading): a combat knife, a pistol, a heavy pistol, an Uzi, a suppressed SMG, a pump shotgun, a sawn-off, an assault rifle, a carbine and a sniper rifle. They come from the owner's Silo game (the Quaternius Ultimate Guns Pack, CC0) with the owner's real firing and handling recordings: 9 mm, .308 and 20-gauge reports, and the right magazine, bolt, slide and shell sounds through each reload.
  - **Choosing**: the weapon button (or X, or LB on a pad) opens the weapon wheel; 1-0 and - pick one directly; Fists puts it away. The Hit button becomes Fire (Slash with the knife); R or the pad's left stick button reloads. The ammo count shows by the buttons.
  - **Free aim** (v0.38, the default; Settings → Aiming): hold **Aim** (the button left of Fire, K or the right mouse button, L2 on a pad) and the gun comes up, the camera comes in over your right shoulder and the view slows for fine aiming; on touch, a quick tap on Aim keeps it up until you tap again, so your thumb is free to drag the view and fire, and a thumb slid on Fire or Aim aims as well. Nothing locks on and nothing snaps: the crosshair stays in the middle of the screen, turns red while it's over a person, and the round goes down the crosshair's line, so what's under it is what's hit. (It used to fly from the muzzle, a hand's width off that line, so a shot aimed just past someone could clip them.) The tracer still leaves the muzzle, and anything in the first couple of metres in front of the gun stops the round, so you can't shoot through a wall you're pressed against. Woodland canopy batches cast shadows only on High (in quarter-size cells there, so the shadow pass draws only the nearby ones); shrubs thin out past 60% of the tree distance on Medium; roaming wildlife is capped at 30 / 50 / 90 (it was the most expensive simulation per frame).
* **Streaming like GTA** (v0.34, `src/perf.js`, `src/tiles.js`, `src/main.js` fog): the world is never loaded whole. It streams in 256 m squares round you: full detail (buildings with shopfronts, trees, street furniture, parked cars) within 240 / 280 / 380 m on Low / Medium / High, simple stand-ins (terrain every 16 m, plain blocks for buildings) out to 520 / 700 / 950 m, and nothing beyond. Over the last third of that distance the haze thickens until the land melts into the sky, so you don't see where it stops (GTA San Andreas does the same with its fog and draw distance). Medium used to keep about 60 squares loaded, out to 1.1 km; now it's about 25. Cars, signs, road markings and street lamps are drawn over shorter distances to match. The brothers' Range Rovers (85,000 triangles each) give way to the ordinary low-detail SUV beyond 35 m, as GTA swaps a car for its low-detail model, and cast their shadow from a simple box; and on Medium the scene no longer writes a screen-sized normals buffer that only High's effects read. Measured in a test browser on the starting street on Medium: 1.18 million triangles a frame before this pass, 0.75 million after (2.85 million in v0.32).
* **Night streets** (v0.33, `src/lampGlow.js`; after GTW, github.com/galatolofederico/gtw, MIT): beyond where the lamp posts themselves are drawn, every street lamp in the loaded world is a warm glow at its lantern, so at night the roads show as chains of lights to the edge of the map. One draw call, nothing by day.
* **Weather in Dev** (v0.33): choosing a weather in the Dev panel holds it: rain stops within a few seconds of choosing Clear and doesn't come back, however long you play, until you pick **Auto**. Before, the choice faded in over about 50 seconds and the timeline took over again after three minutes, so it could rain again.
* **Police** (v0.40, `src/wanted.js`, as GTA V's wanted level): a patrol car on each of six beats plus foot patrols, attending crashes, breakdowns and people hurt (`src/police.js`). **Only killing someone brings them after you**: gunfire, a fight, robbing a shop, taking a car or knocking someone down doesn't. A killing seen by an officer or a police car puts them straight on to you; one seen by the public is phoned in (999) a few seconds later and they come to where it happened; nobody saw it, nobody comes. **Stars** (under the minimap): one for a killing and one more for each after (two for an officer), one more for shooting at the police. Up to **four stars** they're out to stop you, not kill you: **Tasers** on foot (you drop for a few seconds and they cuff you), **stingers** (spike strips across the road ahead, from two stars: your tyres are shredded), the **PIT** (a car comes alongside your back wheel and turns in, and your back end comes round), ramming, cutting in front of you, and **boxing you in** when you slow down, front, back and sides, before the crews get out and pull you from the car; from four stars, **roadblocks** of two cars across the road. At **five stars** they bring in everything (seven cars) and **armed response** officers in dark kit, who stand off and shoot: at you, or at your car and through its windows. **Seen and searching**: while a car crew or an officer can see you (a clear line through the town, about 110 m from a car, 75 m on foot) they come straight for you; out of sight for a few seconds the stars flash and they search round where you were last seen (the flashing circle on the minimap). Get out of the circle and stay unseen (indoors counts) and they lose you. The cars come by the roads on blue lights (traffic pulls over) and, once they're close with a clear line to you, are driven off their lanes through the same car physics as yours, so a ram or a PIT really shoves you (and for a moment overpowers your stability control, as a real PIT does). Stop, get Tasered, give yourself up (Use, when officers are close and there's nothing to get into) or get boxed in: **BUSTED**, out at the nearest police station (Reading, Loddon Valley, Wokingham or Bracknell) with a £150 fine. **Health** (the green bar): police rounds hurt and it comes back slowly once they stop; at nothing you're **WASTED** and wake at the Royal Berkshire or Wokingham Hospital with a £250 bill. Dev panel → Police: wanted level up, clear, heal (to try it without killing anyone). `tools/test/wanted.mjs` drives the scenarios in the game.
* **Emergency services** (v0.40, `src/emergency.js`), all over the map, for anyone, not just what you did:
  - **Ambulances** come to everyone left lying hurt or dead: shot and gone down, knocked out in a fight, knocked down by a car, hurt in a crash, killed. Someone hurt stays down until they come (a witness phones 999; nobody comes after you for a wounding, so it goes to the ambulance service, not the police). The ambulance comes on blue lights and pulls up near them; the two paramedics (green, in yellow-and-green chequered hi-vis) go round to the back, open the doors, draw out the wheeled stretcher at the deck's height, lower it on to its wheels and pump it up; one pushes, the other guides it over and they park it alongside the patient; they kneel and treat them, lift them on, wheel them back, swing it square to the doors and load it in head first (its legs folding), shut the doors, get back in and drive off (on blue lights if the patient's alive). A body stays where it fell until they've taken it away. Up to four ambulances are out at once.
  - **The fire brigade** comes to a car on fire (one smashed up badly enough by crashes or gunfire smokes, then goes up; now and then a parked car just catches) and to a crash with someone trapped. The engine stops short; three firefighters (dark tunics with yellow and silver bands) go to the lockers by the pump on the fire's side; one runs the hose out to the fire with the branch, the second backs him up on the line, the third stays on the pump; the jet arcs on to the car (water you can see, spray where it lands, steam off the fire, and the hiss of it: the rain recording, positioned at the car), the flames shrink and go out, they damp it down, make the hose up and drive off, leaving a blackened shell (cleared away once you're well clear). For someone trapped they work at the car with the cutting gear while the paramedics see to the driver.
  - The police wait at a scene until the paramedics have finished. Dev panel → Emergency response: injury crash, trapped occupant, vehicle fire, **Casualty nearby** (knocks out the nearest person, for the ambulance to come to).
* **Gore** (v0.41, `src/gore.js`, the wounds drawn by `src/humans.js`): a round through someone throws blood out of the exit side in a cone of droplets (fewer back towards the gun), with a mist that hangs a moment; the droplets fly, fall and leave spots where they land; there's a splash up the wall behind them (drips running down it) or across the ground, shaped by the way it flew; the wound itself shows on their clothes or skin where it went in and soaks outwards (two wounds a person); a body lies in a pool that spreads for half a minute; someone getting away hurt leaves drips. Head shots and shotguns throw more. How they die depends on the hit (`src/hitReactions.js`): a head shot drops them where they stand; a shotgun or a big round close in throws them off their feet; others go over backwards, down on to their knees and forward on to their face, or spin round and down sideways, sometimes with a stagger first and a twitch or two after. A knife leaves a short arc of blood and a wound.
* **Taking vehicles** (v0.41, `src/carjack.js`): any car on the road with someone at the wheel that's stopped or crawling (traffic in a queue, at the lights; every model, the supplied BMW X3 and M3, Volvo S60 and Mercedes EQC included) and any bike, e-bike, moped, scooter or motorbike with someone on it: Use (**Steal car**, **Take bike**...) and the brother runs to the driver's door (the right: British cars), yanks it open and drags the driver out on to the road, gets in and drives off; or boots the rider off and gets on. They pick themselves up and run. Not buses, police cars, ambulances, fire engines, a taxi on a job, or the brothers' own cars.
* **Bikes in a crash** (v0.41, `src/carjack.js`): run into someone on a bike, moped or motorbike and they're thrown off the way the car was going (hurt badly from about 20 mph, and the ambulance comes; killed by you from 36 mph); the bike goes over on its side and slides and spins to a stop in a spray of sparks. Pick it up and ride it if you like.
* **Crashes and damage** (v0.41, `src/carPhysics.js`): the dents are as before, but what makes a car smoke (and in the end burn) is its engine (`v.eng`), worn mostly by hits to the front: it takes three or four hard crashes, not one. A car you hit takes it as if braced (very little bounce, its wheels locked and scrubbing), so it lurches a few metres and slews to a stop instead of shooting off down the road, and the front of yours crumples and takes much of your speed; big hits throw up dust and radiator steam and stretch the moment for a split second.
* **Taxis and Kerb** (v0.40, `src/taxis.js`, `src/phone.js`): cabs (black, white and silver hackney carriages with the lit TAXI sign on the roof, about one car in six in a town centre) work the streets. One coming along towards you: Use (**Hail taxi**) and it pulls in beside you; get in the back (Use) and choose where to on the phone. Or book on the **phone** (D-pad up, N, or the Phone button; Circle or Back to come out): **Kerb** offers your waypoint, home and the nearest pubs, food, shops, stations, petrol stations, districts and landmarks, with the distance by road, the time and the fare (£3.20 to start, £1.75 a km, £5 at least); Request Kerb and a driver is sent from a few streets away (their name, the car and its Reading registration on the phone, the car green on the minimap); it pulls up beside you with its hazards on; Use gets you in the back. It drives you there through the traffic, with the camera behind it; **Stop here** (Use, or the phone) lets you out at the next bit of kerb for what's on the meter, and **Skip the journey** takes you straight there. You're let out on the kerb side and the fare comes out of your cash. No driver picks you up while the police are after you. The phone also has the Road Atlas, the radio (when there's one to hand) and the bank (both brothers' cash and your last rides).
* **Weather and light**: one clock and weather timeline for the whole map, saved across swaps and relaunches. Raymarched clouds are lit by the same sun, cast moving shadows on the streets, and drift with the wind. Rain is kept out from under roofs, canopies and shelters. Roads darken and turn reflective, with puddles and light streaks, then dry gradually. Dawn, dusk and night drive the street lamps, lit windows and shop fronts, and vehicle lights.
* **Sound**: recorded and spatial. Rain changes under a canopy or inside a car; there's wind and leaves, engines and wet-tyre hiss, footsteps by surface, crowd murmur, crossing beeps, horns and two-tone sirens.

## Build

```bash
npm install                 # three 0.186.1, esbuild
npm run build               # src/ -> www/app.js + www/worker.js
npm run serve               # http://localhost:8080
npm run apk                 # signed dev APK -> builds/ (needs JDK 17+ and Android SDK 35; see tools/android/build.sh)
npm run test                # unit tests (node --test, tools/test/unit)
bash tools/shadercheck/run.sh   # every material on WebGL2 (GLSL errors) and WebGPU (WGSL validated with naga, vertex-buffer limits)
```

### Rebuilding the world data

```bash
pip install osmium pyproj shapely numpy pillow
# put uk_os_OSTN15_NTv2_OSGBtoETRS.tif (cdn.proj.org) in pyproj's data dir for OSTN15 transforms
mkdir -p data && curl -o data/south_east.osm.pbf https://download.openstreetmap.fr/extracts/europe/united_kingdom/england/south_east-latest.osm.pbf
python3 tools/pipeline/extract_osm.py data/south_east.osm.pbf data/berks.osm.pbf
python3 tools/pipeline/fetch_terrain.py data          # EA LIDAR DTM via WCS, 4 m
npm run world                                          # tiles, network, overview map, manifest
python3 tools/pipeline/verify.py data www/world docs/verification.json
python3 tools/pipeline/report.py                       # docs/COVERAGE.md
```

### Acceptance run

`xvfb-run -a node tools/test/acceptance.js docs/captures` (with `npm run serve` running) drives the game with real touch events. It launches into Woodley, walks into buildings, crosses at a signal, watches a bus and a pedestrian at Cemetery Junction, parks and swaps brothers, drives under the A3290, cycles the weather, waits out a police response, resumes from a save and checks that no mission UI appears. It writes screenshots, a video and `acceptance.json`.
