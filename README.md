# Green County · Berkshire sandbox (v0.22.0)

A free-roam, 1:1 slice of Berkshire, 219 km² from Reading and Caversham in the west to Bracknell in the east, plus the whole M4 across it from Junction 12 (Theale) to Junction 8/9 (Holyport): Reading town centre, Cemetery Junction, Earley, Woodley, Winnersh, Wokingham and Bracknell, and every village, lane, footpath, lake and river between them (Sonning, Twyford, Charvil, Hurst, Dinton Pastures, Sindlesham, Shinfield, Arborfield, Binfield and more). It starts in **sandbox mode**: you control Cal or Leon Ward and can explore indefinitely. There are no missions, objectives, phone calls, deliveries or sales. The cannabis-empire layer from the v0.3–v0.5 prototype will sit on this world later; its save data is carried along untouched.

## Play

* **Android**: install `builds/Green-County-0.22.0-dev.apk`. It was built in another container from v0.21.1 and signed with that container's development key (SHA-256 2e5c0581…, the key of v0.19.0 and v0.21.0), not the original key v0.20.x and v0.21.1 used, so uninstall those first (saved games on the device go with it); v0.19.0 and v0.21.0 update in place. Landscape only.
* **Touch**: on foot, the left thumb walks (a stick appears wherever you touch) and the right drags the camera; Run, Jump, Hit and Pick up sit bottom right, with Get in / Board / Exit above them. Driving, the left thumb steers: Settings → Steering offers a **Steering wheel** you drag sideways (the default; proportional, as Real Racing's and CarX's wheels), **Buttons** (left and right, as GTA's and Real Racing's touch zones: a tap is a small correction, a hold full lock, and letting go centres the wheel) or **Tilt**. Settings → Handling: **Easy** (assists on, the default) or **Realistic** (fewer assists). The right thumb works the GAS and BRAKE pedals (rock between them without lifting), plus Handbrake and Horn. Holding BRAKE at a standstill engages reverse after a moment.
* **Browser**: `npm run serve`, then open http://localhost:8080. Chrome or Edge recommended; WebGPU is used when available, otherwise WebGL2.

Touch controls (landscape): drag on the **left side** to move (a floating stick appears under your thumb). Driving: push the stick up for throttle, pull it back to brake and then reverse, and steer sideways. Drag on the **right side** to orbit the camera. **Run** (on foot) doubles as the **handbrake** in a vehicle (it locks the rear wheels, so it swings the tail round at speed). **Get in / Exit** works on any parked car. **Horn** appears while driving. **Swap** switches between Cal and Leon wherever each is. **Map** opens the full map, **Dev** the developer panel.
Keyboard: WASD or arrows, Shift run, F get in/out, Space handbrake, H horn, C/V camera, Tab swap, M map, P dev panel, Q/E orbit.
Controller (DualShock or any standard-mapping pad): left stick moves and steers, right stick looks. Driving: R2 throttle, L2 brake and reverse, Cross/Square/L1 handbrake, R1 camera view, Circle or L3 horn. On foot: Cross run, Square jump, Circle/R2 hit, L1/R1 pick up a parcel. Triangle gets in or out, boards a bus or train, and requests the next stop. Options or PS opens the menu, and Share or the touchpad opens the Road Atlas. On the Atlas, the stick pans, L1/R1 zoom and Triangle drops a waypoint. D-pad: up opens the Atlas, down swaps brothers, left/right turn the camera 90°. R3 recentres the camera. In menus, the d-pad moves, Cross selects and Circle goes back.
Start menu: **Enter free roam**, **Road Atlas** (search places, drop a pin anywhere and follow the route on the minimap and in-car sat nav) and **Settings** (minimap orientation and size, route colour).

The developer panel can set the hour and clock speed, force any weather (clear, broken, overcast, drizzle, rain, mist, clearing), soak or dry the roads, stage a collision for the police to attend, run extra simulation steps, teleport to districts and test spots, show the performance overlay, save a performance log (written to app storage on Android), and switch between WebGPU and WebGL2.

URL options (browser): `?backend=webgl|webgpu`, `?quality=low|medium|high`, `?perf` (overlay), `?time=17.5`, `?weather=rain`, `?scale=120`, `?sim=4`, `?r0=…&r1=…` (streaming radii), `?post=0`, `?ao=0`, `?ibl=0`, `?grass=0|1`, `?ssr=0`, `?fsr=0` (no upscaling), `?skybake=0` (per-pixel sky), `?tm=aces` (look A/B), `?nobatch` (render batching off).

## What runs

* **World**: 3,730 streamed 256 m tiles (the whole study area and a 1 km corridor along the M4 from J12 to J8/9, 219 km²) in two LOD rings, built from OSM and EA LIDAR. Each building footprint becomes its own closed mesh with gabled or hipped roofs, chimneys, procedural façades, front doors, shopfronts and lit windows. Carriageways, kerbed pavements, paths and plazas are seamless polygons; markings include centre and lane lines, stop and give-way lines, zebras with zig-zags, and studs at signalised crossings. Bridges and flyovers have decks, parapets and piers on separate levels. Also streamed: street lamps, trees, walls, hedges, fences, shop fascias, rails, bus stops and shelters, canopies, the Cemetery Junction gateway and Reading Town Hall.
* **Roads** (`src/roadProfile.js`, `src/roadDetail.js`, applied when the network loads): every bridge deck spans between the embankment crests in the LIDAR ground and rises only for headroom over the road or railway beneath. Roads climb to a deck at their class's grade (motorways 3 %), on earth embankments with abutments. Holes in the ground under roads (culverts, underpasses) are filled. The M4's dropped River Loddon spans are rejoined, and one-way carriageways that leave the map no longer send traffic back the wrong way. Motorways have a hard shoulder and 3.65 m lanes, a concrete step barrier in the central reservation and red/amber/white studs. Dual carriageways get a steel safety fence, and so do the verges of high embankments. Markings follow TSRGD: hazard lines before junctions, single broken give-way lines at roundabouts, broken motorway edge lines where a slip road joins or leaves, and zig-zags only at stand-alone crossings. Signal stop lines and heads come only from traffic arriving at a signal. The drawn road always matches the height traffic drives at: decks carry on as approach slabs while a road leaving a bridge stands above the ground, the ground is cut down where it stands above a road (underpasses, the gaps between bridge spans, cuttings narrower than the 4 m terrain grid), and a deck carries any piece of road the ground under it falls away from (a slip road climbing beside a cutting); the player drives on those decks too. Bridge piers stand clear of the roads, rivers and railways beneath. Bus stops mapped in the carriageway stand at the kerb on the side their buses use, with a BUS STOP box where buses pass; every flag and shelter is fitted onto the drawn footway, clear of the road and of buildings (`npm run stops` → `www/world/stops.json`).
* **Trains and stations** (`src/trains.js`, `src/stations.js`, `tools/rail/build_rail.mjs` → `www/world/rail.json`): the Reading–Waterloo line (Reading, Earley, Winnersh Triangle, Winnersh, Wokingham, Bracknell, Martins Heron) and the Reading–Basingstoke line (Reading, Reading West) run trains every few minutes on the left-hand track each way, stopping at every station and reversing at the ends of the map. The Great Western main line east of Reading has four tracks, paired by use: stopping trains run on the relief (slow) lines on the north side between Reading and Twyford, calling at both, and fast trains run non-stop on the main lines on the south side through Reading and Twyford to the edges of the map; on every line the up (towards London) and down services each keep to their own left-hand track, and no two lines share a track. Every station has a lit platform beside each running track (one at a terminus) with tactile paving, a yellow line, shelter, benches, lamps and name boards, level with the train's floor; where a station stands in a cutting the ground is cut back down to the track under its platforms. Walk onto a platform and press Triangle (or Board) when a train stands there to get on; the doors open on the platform side, the cabin shows the next station, Triangle requests the stop, and at the end of the line everyone gets off. Waiting passengers board and alight too.
* **Procedural detail and street life**: houses get canted bay windows (some two-storey), door hoods on brackets, fascias, soffits, gutters and downpipes, bargeboards, ridge tiles, chimney pots and a plinth. Streets get wheelie bins, telegraph poles with line wires and drops to the houses, pillar boxes, litter bins and benches. All of it is built inside the existing tile meshes, so it adds no draw calls.
* **Image and frame rate**: the scene renders at a dynamic internal resolution and is upscaled with FSR 1 (edge-adaptive upsampling + sharpening), so the image stays sharp while the scale moves to hold 60 fps (30 fps evenly paced as the floor). The cloud sky is baked into a cube map four times a second instead of raymarched per pixel; cars beyond 70 m use a light LOD.
* **A steady picture** (v0.21): nothing should shimmer or flicker as you move.
  - **Shadows**: the sun's shadow map follows you in whole shadow-map texels, measured in the light's own frame (`main.js placeSun`). It used to slide under the world by fractions of a texel every frame, so every shadow edge crawled as you walked or drove. It now also turns with the sun in quarter-degree steps rather than a little every frame. The normal offset is two shadow texels instead of 0.6 m, so shadows meet the feet and wheels that cast them, and bins, bollards and animals cast shadows again.
  - **Windows and shopfronts**: frames, glazing bars, sills, doors, shop mullions, fascias and cladding ribs are drawn per pixel. They are now anti-aliased against the pixel's footprint on the wall, and the rooms seen through distant windows settle to their average colour. Before, detail thinner than a pixel snapped on and off as the camera moved.
  - **Ambient occlusion**: GTAO's noise pattern is smoothed by two depth-aware blur passes, so contact shadows no longer crawl.
  - **Reflections**: screen-space reflections are kept to surfaces facing up (wet roads, puddles, bonnets). They shimmered on upright glass.
  - **Trees**: trees fade out over the last 70 m before the tree draw distance, instead of whole tiles of them popping on the skyline.
  - **Resolution**: dynamic resolution only sharpens again after two quiet checks in a row, in small steps.
* **Buildings measured from LIDAR**: every footprint's wall height, roof type (flat, gabled, hipped), roof height and ridge direction come from the Environment Agency LIDAR surface model (OGL), registered to the OSM footprints block by block (`tools/pipeline/lidar_buildings.py`, summary in `docs/COVERAGE.md`).
* **Driving and crashes** (`src/carPhysics.js`): each car is a rigid body on slip-angle tyres, with weight transfer, an engine power curve and automatic gearbox (5–7 gears), drag, engine braking and a rear-locking handbrake. Grip drops on wet roads, gravel and grass, and a tyre grips a little less per kilogram the harder it is loaded. Driving assists, as on the best mobile racers (Real Racing 3's steering assist and traction control, Gran Turismo's counter-steer assist) and on every modern road car (v0.22): the steering never takes more lock than the front tyres can use at that speed, turns more calmly at speed and centres quickly; ABS and brake-force distribution keep every wheel turning under braking; traction control gives each driven axle only what its tyres can put down while holding the corner; stability control pulls back a car turning faster than the steering asks; and the chassis understeers a little at the limit. On **Easy** (the default) nothing spins however it's driven, dry or wet; **Realistic** lets the car move around more (less help on the sports cars). Before, braking or lifting off in a corner swapped ends in most cars, the rear-drive cars spun under power, and full lock at speed scrubbed at about half a g (`tools/test/unit/handling.test.mjs`). Collisions are impulses at the contact point against buildings, walls, big trees and other cars: glancing blows scrape and turn you, struck cars are shoved and spin, and hard hits are reported to the police. Street things give way (`src/breakables.js`, thresholds in `src/breakSpec.js`): hit hard enough, lamp posts and telegraph poles snap and topple (lamps go dark), young trees fall, wheelie bins, litter bins, benches and fence panels are thrown, pillar boxes go over, hedges and shrubs are flattened and garden walls burst into bricks, each taking only part of your speed; nose into them slowly and they stop you. Buildings, mature trees, tall walls and guard rails always stop you. Crashes throw glass, paint chips, plastic and sparks, shed number plates and trim, jolt the body on its springs, leave smashed fronts steaming, and play the biggest hits in a moment of slow motion (`?slowmo=0` turns that off). Four damage zones per car crumple the bodywork, crack glass and break lamps; a smashed front loses power and pulls to one side (Dev panel → Repair). Also: skid marks, tyre smoke, an engine note that follows rpm, tyre squeal and scrapes, sprung bodies that dive and roll, a speed-widened camera and impact shake. `tools/test/unit/carphysics.test.mjs` checks 0–60 times, top speeds, braking distances (wet and dry), cornering, handbrake turns, crashes and tunnelling.
* **The brothers' Range Rover** (`src/heroCars.js`, `www/vehicles/rangerover.glb`): Cal's and Leon's own cars are Range Rovers, parked at the start; either brother can drive either one, and Dev → Range Rover delivers another.
  - The model is the supplied one, with the wheels on their real axles: they roll with the distance driven and the fronts steer.
  - Handling: permanent four-wheel drive, a 330 kW V8 through eight gears and 2.45 t on air suspension; about 4.5 s to 60 mph and 150 mph flat out.
  - Lights are lit per pixel where the model's lenses are: headlights with DRLs, tail and brake lights, the high-level brake light, reversing lights and indicators.
  - The glass is glossy and tinted, and crashes crumple, scuff and break the lamps.
  - Sound: a real V8 (CC0 recordings) rebuilt into rpm-matched loops, with its own start-up and throttle blips.
* **People and cars**: one continuous, procedurally skinned body per person (eight canvas-baked faces with eyes, lashes, brows, stubble, beards, lips and age lines; strand-textured hair on hairline-following shells; clothing, hats, hoods, umbrellas, hi-vis), animated walking, running and posing on the GPU; detailed cars (hatch, hot hatch, saloon, estate, SUV, van, coupé, mid-engined sports car, police). Bodies are lofted shells with flat sides, a shoulder crease and a realistic cabin. Glass with pillars, swept headlights with projector bowls and LED strips, wraparound tail lights (brake, reversing, indicators), grilles, badges, shut lines and road grime are drawn per pixel. Cars also have UK plates, mirrors, cockpits, alloys and contact shadows, and AI cars indicate before turns.
* **Cameras**: the Cam button (or C) cycles Chase, Far chase, High, Driver and Cinematic while driving, and Close, Far and High on foot.
* **Water** (`build_world.py`, `materials.js: waterMaterial`): every mapped lake, pond, river, canal and open brook. Levels come from the LIDAR DTM (flat over open water); beds are carved below them, deeper away from the shore. The surface is lit by hand: wind-driven waves, rain rings, Fresnel sky and street-light reflections, sun glitter, clear shallows over the bed and dark peaty depths, lapping at the shore. Wade in and you slow down; past chest depth you swim (splashes, a spreading wake, no jumping); drive in and the water drags the car to a stop and floods the engine.
* **Wildlife, farm animals and pets** (`src/wildlifeSpecies.js` rules, `src/wildlifeSim.js` behaviour, `src/wildlifeModels.js` animated low-poly models drawn one instanced mesh per kind, `src/wildlife.js` glue; sources in `docs/SOURCES.md`). Forty-seven kinds recorded in Berkshire, each where and when it would really be. Who is about follows the land cover, water, trees, how busy the place is (pedestrian footfall) and the hour, set by the sun rather than the clock, so 21:30 at midsummer is still dusk.
  - **Rarity**: a fox in a shopping street at midday is vanishingly rare, but foxes are about the gardens at night. A hedgehog by day is rare but possible. Badgers keep further from people than foxes. Rain sends small birds and bats under cover.
  - **Lakes and rivers**: mallards, coots, moorhens, great crested grebes, tufted ducks, mute swans, and Canada, greylag and Egyptian geese. In early summer, ducklings, cygnets, goslings and coot chicks swim in a line behind their mothers. Ducks upend, grebes, coots and tufted ducks dive, geese go up the bank to graze, and they swim over if you stand still at the water's edge. Grey herons fish from the bank at dawn and dusk, and gulls work the water.
  - **Countryside**: herds of cattle (beef herds with calves), sheep (with lambs) and horses (rugged in winter) keep to their pasture. They graze as the herd drifts round the field, lie down to chew the cud at night and around midday, and cows come over to stare if you stand at the fence. Rabbits come out of their warrens at dawn and dusk and bolt back underground. Badgers leave their setts in the woods after dark. Also about: brown hares, pheasants, roe and muntjac deer, rooks cawing round their rookeries, buzzards, kestrels hovering over the verges and red kites circling everywhere.
  - **Gardens, parks and town**: cats doze, sit and roam, and some come over to wind round your legs. Grey squirrels dash up the trees. There are wood pigeons, blackbirds, robins, blue tits, house sparrows, starlings, magpies, crows, jackdaws, green woodpeckers and ring-necked parakeets, and feral pigeons round the crowds. In summer, swifts scream round the rooftops and swallows skim the fields and water. Nests sit in the trees, with a bird sitting tight and its mate flying to and fro with food in spring and early summer.
  - **After dark**: foxes (town foxes let you nearer), hedgehogs snuffling through the gardens (they roll into a ball if you come close), pipistrelle bats over the water and the trees, tawny owls, rats by the water and wood mice. Eyes catch the light.
  - **Dogs**: people walk dogs (labradors, retrievers, spaniels, collies, German shepherds, terriers, Staffies, French bulldogs, dachshunds and more), most in the morning and evening and in the parks. On the lead they trot at heel, stop to sniff and sit when their person stops. Off the lead in the park they range about and chase squirrels and pigeons.
  - **Reactions**: birds go up together when you, a dog or a car come too close, and settle again further off or in a tree. Deer stop and stare, then bolt.
  - **Nothing pops up in front of you**: animals passing through arrive out of sight, or far enough off not to notice, and birds fly in. Herds, flocks, warrens, setts and rookeries are in the same places on every visit.
  - **Calls**: the simulation knows when each animal calls (the dawn chorus, quacks and honks, the wood pigeon's coo, crows and rooks, the red kite's whistle, the tawny owl's hoot, barks, moos and bleats), and they will play once real recordings are in the sound bank; the game plays real recorded sound only.
  - `tools/test/unit/wildlife.test.mjs` checks the rules on a made-up map (`wildworld.mjs`): rarity, the hours, the places, nothing appearing in view, and dogs with their people.
* **Brothers**: Cal and Leon are the only playable characters. Each keeps his own outfit, position, heading and vehicle across swaps and relaunches. You can walk, run, drive, park and get out (on the driver's side). Collisions apply against buildings, vehicles and people.
* **Traffic**: vehicles have stable IDs and persistent state, follow A*-routed journeys, keep to UK left-hand lanes, car-follow with IDM, obey signals, give way at junctions and roundabouts, don't block junction boxes, stop for zebras, and change lanes or overtake round stopped vehicles. Buses run the OSM bus routes and serve stops and queues. The bubble is simulated at full rate near you and more cheaply further out, without despawning anything in view.
* **People**: pedestrians walk pavements with destinations and time-of-day and district density. They walk in groups, chat, queue at bus stops, cross at signals (pressing the button) and zebras, react to horns, collisions, disturbances and rain (umbrellas, hurrying, sheltering), and get knocked down and back up (non-graphic).
* **Police**: a patrol car on each of six beats plus foot patrols. Witnessed incidents are phoned in; the nearest unit responds under blue lights while traffic pulls over, attends, clears and resumes patrol. If you cause trouble in sight of officers you get a proportionate stop (words of advice) or a pursuit, which ends cleanly (lost them, or detained and released) and hands control back.
* **Weather and light**: one clock and weather timeline for the whole map, saved across swaps and relaunches. Raymarched clouds are lit by the same sun, cast moving shadows on the streets, and drift with the wind. Rain is kept out from under roofs, canopies and shelters. Roads darken and turn reflective, with puddles and light streaks, then dry gradually. Dawn, dusk and night drive the street lamps, lit windows and shop fronts, and vehicle lights.
* **Sound**: synthesised and spatial. Rain changes under a canopy or inside a car; there's wind and leaves, engines and wet-tyre hiss, footsteps by surface, crowd murmur, crossing beeps, horns and two-tone sirens.

## Build

```bash
npm install                 # three 0.186.1, esbuild
npm run build               # src/ -> www/app.js + www/worker.js
npm run serve               # http://localhost:8080
npm run apk                 # signed dev APK -> builds/ (needs JDK 17+ and Android SDK 35; see tools/android/build.sh)
npm run test                # unit tests (node --test, tools/test/unit)
bash tools/shadercheck/run.sh   # every material on WebGL2 (GLSL errors) and WebGPU (WGSL validated with naga, vertex-buffer limits)
```

### Rebuilding the world data

```bash
pip install osmium pyproj shapely numpy pillow
# put uk_os_OSTN15_NTv2_OSGBtoETRS.tif (cdn.proj.org) in pyproj's data dir for OSTN15 transforms
mkdir -p data && curl -o data/south_east.osm.pbf https://download.openstreetmap.fr/extracts/europe/united_kingdom/england/south_east-latest.osm.pbf
python3 tools/pipeline/extract_osm.py data/south_east.osm.pbf data/berks.osm.pbf
python3 tools/pipeline/fetch_terrain.py data          # EA LIDAR DTM via WCS, 4 m
npm run world                                          # tiles, network, overview map, manifest
python3 tools/pipeline/verify.py data www/world docs/verification.json
python3 tools/pipeline/report.py                       # docs/COVERAGE.md
```

### Acceptance run

`xvfb-run -a node tools/test/acceptance.js docs/captures` (with `npm run serve` running) drives the game with real touch events. It launches into Woodley, walks into buildings, crosses at a signal, watches a bus and a pedestrian at Cemetery Junction, parks and swaps brothers, drives under the A3290, cycles the weather, waits out a police response, resumes from a save and checks that no mission UI appears. It writes screenshots, a video and `acceptance.json`.
